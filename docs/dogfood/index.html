Same brief, two projects, one of them wired.

Two throwaway Next.js apps got the identical brief with no hint that a component registry existed. Repo A was a bare shadcn init. Repo B had the six registries and @jhai/theme-base. Two isolated sessions, neither able to see the other's work.

The number that matters is not the line count

B wrote half as much code. That is the smaller half of the result.

Repo A cannot be rebranded. Its 320 lines contain 58 hardcoded palette utilities and zero semantic tokens — bg-white, text-slate-900, bg-blue-600. Changing a CSS variable moves nothing. Every client would be a find-and-replace through five component files.
Repo B restyles from the token block. Zero palette utilities in the hand-written code, 13 semantic tokens, and every installed component reads through --jh-*. The whole page follows a client's palette without a component edit.
Repo A's own report claimed it was rebrandable. It said the page “reskins via CSS variables alone — no component edits needed.” That is false, and it was caught by measuring rather than by asking. Worth knowing: an agent's self-assessment of its own restylability is not evidence.

What went against the registry

Reported because it is the honest half of the result.

  • B took 12 minutes to A's 2.5. Searching, installing and reconciling four blocks costs more wall-clock on the first page than typing 320 lines of throwaway JSX. The trade is real: B is slower to first render and cheaper for the rest of the component's life. On a genuinely one-off page, A wins on time.
  • The pricing table — the centre of the brief — still had to be hand-written. The installed mist-pricing-1 turned out to be single-tier enterprise, not three-tier. Seven Tailark pricing blocks were indexed and none matched the most common shape a pricing page needs. That is a curation gap worth fixing.
  • B hit three friction points that cost minutes: SVG components missing a <SVGSVGElement> type argument, a hero block expecting a separate header module that had to be shimmed with a one-line re-export, and the overwrite prompts on button.tsx that --yes does not cover.
  • Two hairline literals were still baked into JHAI's own components — border-black/20 in Button and border-black/8 in StatTile. Found by measuring B's output, not by review. Now tokenised as --jh-edge-* and --jh-rule-light.

The call

The registry earns its place on rebrandability, not on speed. A page that cannot take a client's palette without editing five files is not reusable at spec-site volume, and that is exactly what the unwired baseline produced — while reporting the opposite. B is slower on the first page and cheaper on every one after it.

Next: fix the three-tier pricing gap, since the brief's centre piece is the one thing 789 indexed items did not cover.
